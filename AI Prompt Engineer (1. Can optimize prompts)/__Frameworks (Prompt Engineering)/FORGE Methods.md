# FORGE Method — Better AI Prompting

**Goal:** Get better AI results by providing context, clarifying objectives, asking questions, evaluating outputs, and improving the final result.

Instead of giving AI one vague instruction and accepting its first response, use a structured process.

---

## Method 1: FORGE — Creating Better AI Outputs

**Source:** [One Person Empire — YouTube](https://youtu.be/YbrrHwwBv1o?t=186)

This version is useful for creating marketing materials, programming prompts, business plans, websites, and other deliverables.

### F — Feed It Context

Tell AI everything relevant before asking it to perform a task.

Provide three types of information:

1. **Who you are:** Your business, role, products, services, and limitations.
    
2. **Who it's for:** Your target audience, their problems, preferences, and characteristics.
    
3. **What success looks like:** Examples of good work and the standards the output must meet.
    

**Example prompt:**

> I'm launching an online programming course.
> 
> My audience consists of solo business owners with little coding experience. They want practical solutions without technical jargon.
> 
> A successful result should be clear, persuasive, and focused on their needs.
> 
> Use this information as context for the following steps.

### O — Outcome, Not Task

Describe the result you want to achieve, not just the activity you want AI to perform.

**Weak instruction:**

> Write an email announcing my new course.

**Better instruction:**

> Write an email that encourages subscribers who have never purchased from me to click through to my new course page.

The difference:

- **Task:** What AI should produce.
    
- **Outcome:** What you want the output to accomplish.
    

Focus on the outcome first.

### R — Reverse Interview

Before beginning, have AI interview you to clarify anything important.

**Prompt:**

> My goal is [describe your desired outcome].
> 
> Before doing any work, ask me the questions you need answered to achieve this goal effectively.
> 
> Ask one question at a time. Do not begin generating the actual deliverable until I give you permission.

Answer the questions until the requirements are sufficiently clear.

### G — Generate, Then Grade

Don't accept the first answer immediately. Generate alternatives and evaluate them.

**Step 1 — Generate different approaches**

> Give me three genuinely different approaches to achieving this outcome. Each should use a distinct strategy, not simply reword the same idea.

**Step 2 — Evaluate the results**

> Evaluate each approach against the success criteria we established.
> 
> Be critical. Identify the strengths and weaknesses of each approach, explain which is most likely to succeed, and what still needs improvement.

**Step 3 — Improve**

Choose the strongest approach and have AI address the weaknesses.

Repeat if necessary.

### E — Export the Win

Once you've achieved a satisfactory result, turn the successful process into reusable instructions.

**Prompt:**

> This approach worked well.
> 
> Convert everything we learned into a reusable prompt for future projects.
> 
> Include the important context, questions, success criteria, evaluation process, and lessons learned.
> 
> Use placeholders for project-specific information so I can apply this workflow to similar tasks.

**Result:** A reusable prompt that incorporates what worked, instead of requiring you to figure everything out again.

## Method 2: FORGE — Developing and Strengthening Ideas

**Source:** [Tom's Guide — Amanda Caswell](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/i-use-the-forge-method-with-claude-to-turn-rough-ideas-into-finished-work-heres-how-it-works)

This is a different FORGE framework, designed to turn rough thoughts into well-developed arguments, articles, proposals, and presentations.

### F — Frame

**Purpose:** Clarify what you're actually trying to say.

Prompt:

> I have a rough idea: [describe idea].
> 
> Ask me three important questions that will help clarify my intentions. Don't suggest solutions or start writing yet.

Answer the questions before moving forward.

### O — Obstruct

**Purpose:** Challenge your assumptions and identify weaknesses.

Prompt:

> Act as a skeptical critic.
> 
> Identify the weakest parts of my idea, unsupported assumptions, logical gaps, and the strongest arguments against it.
> 
> Don't simply agree with me. Challenge anything that doesn't hold up.

### R — Reconstruct

**Purpose:** Strengthen the idea based on the weaknesses discovered.

Prompt:

> Help me rebuild this idea using the criticism from the previous step.
> 
> Improve the central argument, identify the evidence needed, and organize it into a logical structure.
> 
> Provide a framework rather than a completed draft.

### G — Generate

**Purpose:** Create a structured outline based on the improved idea.

Prompt:

> Develop a detailed outline using the framework we established.
> 
> Audience: [target audience].
> 
> Tone: [desired tone].
> 
> Preserve my original intentions while incorporating the improvements.

Then use the outline to develop your draft.

### E — Edit

**Purpose:** Review and improve the completed work.

Prompt:

> Review my draft in three passes:
> 
> 1. Identify vague, generic, or weak language and suggest improvements.
>     
> 2. Identify the strongest part of the writing and explain why it works.
>     
> 3. Identify important missing information or arguments.
>     
> 
> Then provide an improved final version without changing my intended message.

## Which FORGE Method Should I Use?

|Scenario|Recommended method|
|---|---|
|Building an app or website|Method 1|
|Generating marketing campaigns|Method 1|
|Creating reusable AI prompts|Method 1|
|Writing articles or essays|Method 2|
|Developing business ideas|Method 2|
|Stress-testing an argument|Method 2|
|Preparing a presentation|Method 2|
|Complex projects requiring both creativity and critical thinking|Combine both|

## Main Takeaway

**Method 1:** Context → Outcome → Questions → Generate and evaluate → Save reusable instructions.

**Method 2:** Clarify idea → Challenge assumptions → Rebuild → Create outline → Edit.

Both approaches work because AI is treated as a collaborator rather than simply a machine that produces answers.

**Important:** Don't rush the process. Review the results at each stage and correct the direction before proceeding.
